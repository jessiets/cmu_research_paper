DECISIONS APPROVED NOVEMBER 3, 2026 These replace any conflicting text
below.
- Current paper: the Revised draft.
- Reference condition: one shared reference evidence channel (R) is
  added to every method and also tested alone. Levels are none, exact,
  and degraded. GEO+R is labeled a reference-assisted hybrid.
- Generator: a small custom generator built on Piecemaker's
  interlockingnubs curve code. Ground truth comes only from grid
  positions.
- Boundary wear: a factor with levels 0 and 1 px (reduced Nov 6).
- Visual complexity: seeded solid-color overlays on the same images, at
  0% and 50% (reduced Nov 6).
- Pair rule: each unordered pair has four side relations, and its pair
  score is the best of the four. Flat sides are excluded.
- Quality metrics: average precision and top-1 side accuracy are
  primary. Accuracy is still reported beside the all-negative baseline.
- Runtime and memory: wall-clock runtime by phase, CPU time and CPU
  utilization, peak RSS, and direct array sizes. tracemalloc was
  dropped on Nov 6.
- Candidate solver repositories: none are used as baselines. All
  baselines are written fresh on one code path.

SCOPE REDUCTION APPROVED NOVEMBER 6, 2026 (Decision Log D1) The
experiment covers just enough to answer RQ1 and RQ2 and support the
paper's existing claims.
- Methods: GEO, COLOR, GRAD, HYB-GC (rank fusion), HYB-FILTER
  (filter-then-score), plus REF-ONLY. Texture matching is not tested
  and is future work.
- Sizes: 6 x 9, 12 x 18, 20 x 30. Images: 3 pilot and 20 main.
- Analysis: five comparisons fixed in advance (spec section A7). No
  interaction models and no decision tree; give conditional guidance.

1.  TOPIC AND SCOPE The study investigates how geometric, visual, and
    hybrid features affect pairwise jigsaw puzzle piece matching under
    different information and computational constraints.

The task is pairwise compatibility: given two pieces, estimate whether
they are true neighbors. This is NOT a complete puzzle reconstruction
system.

Current scope assumptions: - Pieces are correctly oriented before
matching. - Global orientation recovery is out of scope. - Full puzzle
assembly, global consistency, and conflict resolution are out of
scope. - Compare pairwise matching strategies, not entire end-to-end
solvers as black boxes. - In reference-free matching, the completed
image is ground truth for the evaluator only; it must not be provided to
the matching method.

2.  APPROVED CLAIM “This research argues that controlled experiments on
    jigsaw puzzle matching can reveal how matching strategies perform
    under different information and computational constraints. These
    findings can then be compared with approaches used in other
    fragment-matching domains to assess whether the same
    strategy-selection principles may apply more broadly.”

Do not claim that the study proves universal cross-domain
generalization. Phase 2 is literature-based, not experimental validation
in other domains.

3.  RESEARCH QUESTIONS RQ1: How do different combinations of geometric
    and visual features affect pairwise-matching accuracy, runtime, and
    memory consumption under different jigsaw puzzle conditions?

RQ2: How does the availability of a completed reference image affect the
relative performance and suitability of different matching strategies?

RQ3: To what extent can observed strategy-selection principles inform
pairwise-matching tasks in other fragment-reconstruction domains, and
what domain-specific constraints limit their applicability?

RQ1 and RQ2 are answered by controlled jigsaw experiments (Phase 1). RQ3
is answered through a structured literature-based comparison (Phase 2).

4.  TWO-PHASE PLAN Phase 1 — Controlled jigsaw experiments:

-   Compare representative geometric, visual, and hybrid pairwise
    matching strategies.
-   Use generated puzzle instances with known ground-truth adjacencies.
-   Evaluate methods on the same instances wherever input requirements
    permit.
-   Compare reference-free and reference-image conditions.
-   Vary puzzle size and visual complexity where feasible.
-   Measure matching quality, runtime, and memory consumption.
-   Use a shared evaluation pipeline and document implementation
    differences.

Phase 2 — Literature-based transferability assessment: - Compare
experimental findings with methods used in other fragment-matching
domains. - Priority domains: fragmented/shredded document reconstruction
and fragmented/torn image reconstruction. - Compare fragment geometry,
visual/structural evidence, reference-information availability, matching
features, computational requirements, and evaluation criteria. -
Identify plausible transferable principles and domain-specific
constraints. - Do not claim experimental validation outside jigsaw
puzzles.

5.  MATCHING STRATEGY FAMILIES Geometric:

-   Compare piece boundary shapes or contours.
-   Possible representations include boundary coordinate sequences or
    curves.
-   For conventional jigsaw pieces, compare complementary/opposing
    boundary profiles.

Visual: - Color: compare corresponding pixel values or color
distributions near candidate boundaries. - Gradient: compare
intensity/color transitions across candidate boundaries. -
Texture/appearance: compare local patterns or broader visual
characteristics near boundaries.

Hybrid: - Combine geometric and visual evidence. - Possible approaches
include normalized score fusion or using one feature to filter candidate
pairs before applying another. - Hybrid does not automatically mean
better; it may add computation, memory use, and implementation
complexity.

These are strategy families, not fully specified algorithms. The
implementation must define the exact representation, feature extraction,
compatibility calculation, score normalization/combination, candidate
generation, and decision threshold/ranking behavior for each method.

6.  EXPERIMENTAL CONDITIONS Information availability: A. Reference-free

-   Completed image is unavailable to the matching algorithm.
-   Evaluator may use the completed image or generator metadata only to
    establish true adjacency and score predictions.
-   Prevent reference-image pixels or derived reference features from
    leaking into the method.

B. Reference-image - Completed image is available to the matching
algorithm. - Document exactly how each strategy uses the reference
image.

Puzzle characteristics to vary where feasible: - Puzzle size / number of
pieces. - Visual complexity, especially varied image content versus
large uniform or solid-color regions. - Potentially image/source
diversity and cutting geometry, subject to feasibility.

Orientation: - Assume all pieces are correctly oriented initially. - Do
not add unknown rotation as an experimental variable unless scope is
explicitly revised.

7.  GROUND TRUTH AND DATA Preferred initial approach:

-   Generate puzzles from known source images using a reproducible
    generator.
-   Preserve piece identity, source-image coordinates, and generator
    metadata so true neighboring pairs can be derived reliably.
-   Use a common set of puzzle instances for all comparable methods.
-   Store a reproducible manifest linking each instance to its source
    image, puzzle size, cutting parameters, piece files, and
    ground-truth adjacency list.

Ground truth: - Represent true adjacency as unordered piece-ID pairs. -
For n pieces, all unique unordered pairs number n(n-1)/2. - In a
conventional rectangular grid with no missing pieces, true neighboring
pairs are far fewer than all possible pairs. Accuracy alone may
therefore be misleading. - Keep ground-truth generation separate from
algorithm input, especially in reference-free experiments.

Representativeness: - Generated puzzles provide controlled ground truth
but may not represent all real puzzles. - Real puzzles may include
irregular cuts, manufacturing variation, damaged pieces, glare, scanning
noise, and other physical imperfections. - Document source-image
diversity, piece count, cutting geometry, visual complexity, and
generation settings. - Real puzzle data may supplement generated data if
suitable data can be obtained and evaluated consistently; this is not a
confirmed requirement.

Generator decision: use Piecemaker's curve code in a small custom
generator (see the approved decisions above and spec section B2).
CV-Jigsaw-Reconstruction was rejected. Piecemaker:
https://github.com/jkenlooper/piecemaker

-   RL Puzzle Solver: https://github.com/RePAIRProject/RL_puzzle_solver

-   jigsaw-solver: https://github.com/bxntn/jigsaw-solver
-   Zolver: https://github.com/Kawaboongawa/Zolver

None are selected as baselines. Zolver, PuzzleSolver, and jigsaw-solver
may inform the design, but code from repositories with no license file
must not be reused.

Do not compare complete solvers and attribute all differences to
geometric vs visual features. Prefer extracting or implementing
comparable pairwise matching components and running them through one
evaluator. If complete systems must be used, document system-level
confounds.

9.  FAIR-COMPARISON REQUIREMENTS Where feasible:

-   Use identical puzzle instances and ground truth across methods.
-   Use a shared evaluator and consistent definition of a predicted
    match.
-   Keep hardware, operating environment, and execution conditions
    consistent.
-   Define timing boundaries consistently.
-   Separate feature extraction time from pairwise comparison time when
    practical.
-   Document whether a method scores all pairs or filters candidates
    first.
-   Document feature representation, score calculation, normalization,
    score fusion, thresholds, and tie handling.
-   Record implementation language, dependencies, configuration, and
    relevant optimizations.
-   Repeat runs where needed to account for timing variability.
-   Prevent ground-truth metadata or reference-image information from
    leaking into reference-free matching.

10. METRICS Primary metrics:

-   Matching quality.
-   Runtime.
-   Memory consumption.

Matching quality: - Compare predicted neighboring pairs with
ground-truth adjacency pairs. - Report accuracy, but consider precision,
recall, and F1-score because true adjacent pairs may be rare relative to
all possible pairs. - Final metric definitions depend on whether a
method ranks all pairs, predicts a fixed number of neighbors, or
classifies pairs using a threshold. Define this consistently. - Top-k or
ranking-based measures are possible only if they fit the actual
algorithm output; not yet selected.

Runtime: - Define exactly what is timed (feature extraction, pairwise
score calculation, or complete matching operation). - Record puzzle size
and number of candidate pairs with every measurement. - Use consistent
execution conditions and repeat runs as appropriate.

Memory: - Measure memory used during matching, including feature
representations, intermediate results, and stored compatibility scores
where practical. - Document dense all-pairs storage versus filtering or
sparse storage. - Do not assume pair-score storage is the dominant cost;
measure it in context.

11. ANALYSIS PLAN

-   Start with descriptive summaries for each strategy and condition.
-   Compare methods across reference availability, puzzle size, and
    visual complexity.
-   Use paired comparisons when methods are tested on the same instances
    and the data support paired analysis.
-   Select statistical tests after the pilot and data inspection; do not
    preselect a test without understanding the experimental structure
    and distributions.
-   Report effect sizes and confidence intervals where feasible.
-   Examine trade-offs among accuracy, runtime, and memory rather than
    ranking by accuracy alone.
-   The final artifact may be a decision tree if results support clear,
    reproducible decision boundaries. Otherwise, provide less
    prescriptive strategy-selection guidance. Do not force a decision
    tree.

12. PILOT STUDY — FIRST IMPLEMENTATION MILESTONE Run a small pilot
    before full data collection to verify:

-   A candidate generator can produce usable pieces and reliable
    ground-truth adjacency.
-   Piece IDs and adjacency metadata remain traceable through generation
    and loading.
-   Candidate matching methods can run or be adapted to a common
    interface.
-   Reference-free mode excludes the completed image from algorithm
    inputs.
-   Reference-image mode passes the reference image in a documented way.
-   The evaluator correctly scores predictions against ground truth.
-   Runtime and memory can be measured consistently.
-   Puzzle sizes and instance counts are practical.
-   Visual-complexity categories can be created reproducibly.

Use pilot results to finalize: - Generator and source-image selection. -
Exact algorithms/features. - Puzzle sizes and number of instances. -
Image-complexity categories. - Candidate-pair generation and
prediction/ranking protocol. - Metric definitions. - Repetition counts
and statistical analysis.

13. VALIDITY CONCERNS Construct validity:

-   Accuracy can be misleading under class imbalance.
-   Define predicted neighboring pairs and ensure metrics match
    algorithm output.
-   Define runtime and memory measurement boundaries.

Internal validity: - Feature extraction, candidate filtering, score
storage, implementation language, and optimization may affect results
independently of feature family. - Use a common pipeline and document
unavoidable differences.

External validity: - Generated puzzles may not represent real puzzle
variability. - Document image content, cutting geometry, puzzle size,
and generation procedure. - Limit claims to tested conditions if
real-world data are not included.

Transferability: - Documents can contain text/layout structure not
present in jigsaws. - Irregular image fragments may have incomplete or
noncomplementary boundaries. - Literature comparison identifies
plausible applicability, not proof of cross-domain performance.
